# RateMyDegrees: website and codebase audit

Date: September 23, 2026. Local revision: `2e787d8`.

Status at audit completion: proposed upgrades only. Implementation was subsequently authorized; see ../implementation-2026-09-23.md for delivery and verification status. Findings and original verification notes below are preserved as the pre-change baseline.

## Assessment

The platform's purpose is for students to review their degree and for incoming students to learn from those real experiences. Its catalog and warm visual identity support that purpose. Contributing and reading reviews should be the primary journeys. Broken navigation, inconsistent search, unsupported claims, and review integrity defects undermine those journeys.

Owner clarification: exclude comparisons, shortlists designed for comparison, major-selection guidance, pricing/affordability tools, ROI tools, rankings, and platform endorsements. The platform should apply the same presentation and moderation rules to every school and program. Favorable and critical firsthand experiences are both legitimate; neutral hosting does not require removing negative opinions.

Recommended sequence: protect review privacy and integrity; repair finding, reading, and contributing reviews; consolidate the code; improve review context and transparency. Existing earnings/career features were audited as current functionality, but expanding or promoting them is outside this revised plan. This scope revision does not by itself direct deletion of existing pages or data.

## Scope and evidence

Live browser inspection covered the homepage, program catalog, Computer Science search and detail, degree statistics, Arizona State's Computer Science page, school catalog, University of Florida search and profile, and the unauthenticated review/sign-in journey. Mobile homepage testing used a 390 × 844 viewport; the viewport was reset afterward. Filters and school-specific search were exercised. Policy pages, authenticated review form, admin flows, data imports, authentication, validation, telemetry, schema, styles, and repository structure were inspected in source.

Evidence labels below distinguish **Live** observations, **Code** findings, and **Proposal** product recommendations. Local source and the sampled deployment visibly agree on several defects, but a deployed commit hash was not available.

This is a broad product and source audit, not certification of every database row or a penetration test. No production reviews, reports, votes, or accounts were created. Authenticated end-to-end submission, admin actions, production payload inspection, database configuration/RLS, dependency vulnerability scanning, full browser accessibility testing, and measured Core Web Vitals remain unverified.

## Student review experience — revised scope

| Question a visitor is trying to answer | Current friction | Suggested experience |
|---|---|---|
| “Where are reviews for my degree and school?” | Search is inconsistent, filters are inert, and program names can be unfamiliar. | Search by degree or school with aliases and case-insensitive matching. Show selected school/program context, accurate review counts, and optional credential filters where supported by data. |
| “What was the degree experience like?” | Empty invitations dominate some pages; submitted written answers are not all displayed. | Put real reviews first, covering coursework, workload, teaching, support, and firsthand experiences. Do not invent editorial descriptions to fill missing reviews. |
| “How much context is behind this rating?” | Sparse samples and inconsistent counts can mislead. | Show review count, dates, student/alumni status, and rating definitions. Clearly label aggregates as student-reported opinions and explain when there are too few reviews. |
| “Is the platform promoting a school?” | Rankings language, promotional claims, and unexplained ordering can imply endorsement. | Use the same layouts and rules for all programs. Default browse to alphabetical order and search to transparent relevance; default reviews to newest. No best/worst lists or recommended schools. |
| “Can I share my experience honestly?” | Long required form, lost preselection, uncertain anonymity, and no edit/delete workflow. | Preserve context, allow not-applicable answers, explain anonymity and moderation, and provide My reviews. Accept relevant praise and criticism under the same rules. |
| “What does this page mean?” | “Control,” “Catalog Root,” “UNITID,” and “Explore Path” add jargon. | Use “School type,” “Degree reviews,” and “Read reviews”; keep internal identifiers out of the main experience. |
| “Can I use this on my phone or with a keyboard?” | No mobile nav, tiny muted labels, unnamed icon buttons/inputs, modal without dialog/focus handling, and a cramped homepage search. | Mobile menu; visible labels; proper autocomplete and modal keyboard behavior; readable text and contrast; reduced-motion support; appropriately sized tap targets. Validate with keyboard, screen reader, zoom, and contrast tooling. |

Keep the existing five-review aggregate threshold consistently across programs, with precise language: “Average shown after 5 reviews.” That threshold alone does not establish statistical reliability. Individual reviews remain readable. No reviews means insufficient coverage, not a poor program. Never suppress relevant criticism simply because it reflects badly on a school, or prioritize praise because it reflects well on one.

## Suggested screen upgrades

### Homepage

Proposed headline: **Real students. Real degree experiences.**

Supporting message: **Read what students and graduates say about their degree—or share your own experience.**

Order: compact navigation → clear Read reviews / Write a review entry points → labeled degree/school search → neutral catalog browsing → explanation of anonymity, review counts, and moderation. If recent reviews are shown, select chronologically under one rule, without cherry-picking sentiment or schools.

Reading reviews should not require sign-in. Keep the warm palette, but reduce oversized spacing, all-caps microcopy, italics, and decorative empty cards.

### Major detail

Order: degree name → review count and clear school context → student reviews with school labels → find reviews at a specific school → invitation to contribute. Make any cross-school aggregate scope explicit; do not present it as a school-specific rating.

Use factual names and short navigation explanations. Exclude “who should choose this major,” recommended career paths, and institution endorsements from new content.

### School/program detail

Title: **Computer Science at Arizona State University** with the official campus name as supporting text.

Order: school/program identity → review count → student-reported rating summary when eligible → reviews, newest first → Write a review. Use the same structure everywhere. Empty state: “No student reviews yet” with an invitation to contribute; no inferred judgment about program quality.

### Neutrality and review context

No comparison screens, school rankings, financial tools, or personalized recommendations. Reading filters may use review date and current-student/alumni status. Explain helpful votes as reader feedback, not verification or platform endorsement. Moderate based on published content rules, not whether a review is positive or negative. Review counts measure participation, not quality.

### Review contribution

Preserve selected school/major, explain public anonymity accurately, show short labeled steps, allow not-applicable ratings, and distinguish published from pending submissions. “My reviews” should show status and ownership-checked controls. Display every written answer the form asks users to contribute, or remove unused prompts: current ReviewItem displays fit/challenge but not misconception/differently.

## Discoverability and performance

- Use review-focused metadata and remove rankings/comparison/major-selection promises.
- Prioritize useful canonical review pages in the sitemap. Avoid flooding it with empty combinations; sign-in-required contribution pages need not be discovery targets. Expanding statistics-page discovery is outside the revised scope.
- Add share previews and consistent titles/brand spelling. Preserve existing URL compatibility.
- Measure before claiming performance improvements. Candidate costs include three font loads, model inference, repeated auth/database work, full review lists, and broad telemetry sampling.
- Many internal links use plain anchors. Adopt framework navigation where it improves transitions and prefetch behavior, with measured limits for large catalogs.
- No measured Lighthouse, Core Web Vitals, bundle-size, or conversion baseline was available in this audit.

## Proposed delivery order and acceptance gates

| Phase | Scope | Gate before proceeding |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Trust and correctness | Public review data shape, ID validators, auth destination validation, moderation consistency, banned-user enforcement, honest claims. | Fixture tests cover authorization/IDs; public response audit verifies anonymity; unsupported claims removed. |
| 2 — Repair core journeys | Mobile nav, working filters, consistent search, accurate cards, review prefill, submission states, accessible controls. | Incoming student reaches relevant real reviews on mobile; reviewer returns from sign-in with context intact. |
| 3 — Maintainability | Remove verified dead code/packages, unify types/validation/search/UI, reduce oversized form, document imports, add CI checks. | Lint, types, build and focused behavior tests pass; no route or data behavior regressions. |
| 4 — Review clarity and neutrality | Review-first pages, consistent rating definitions and coverage, readable full feedback, neutral catalog ordering and moderation explanations. | Same treatment across schools; no rankings, financial tools, or recommendations; reading remains public; positive and negative experiences follow identical moderation rules. |

Track successful search-to-review visits, empty/error search rates, review completion by step, moderation turnaround, and edit/delete success. Establish a baseline first; do not invent uplift or conversion forecasts. Do not optimize for positive sentiment or promotion of particular schools.
